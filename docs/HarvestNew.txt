PERIODIC HARVEST AND UPDATE
===========================
Written for a human. This is the every-few-months refresh: new citations,
new repositories, new people, and the summaries that need revisiting
because of them.


HOW OFTEN
---------
Quarterly is the right cadence. Citations accrue slowly and the indexes
themselves lag by weeks, so a monthly run costs the same effort and returns
very little new. Run it monthly only in the months after a paper lands, when
early citations matter to you.


HOW TO RUN IT
-------------
Open a session in ~/workspace/nextgen and say:

    harvest and update

That phrase is the trigger. The full plan lives in
tasks/HARVEST-AND-UPDATE.md — eight phases, from intake through to the live
site — and every settled ruling is already written down there, so you should
not have to re-answer questions you have answered before.

Work runs in parallel lanes with separate file ownership; the pattern is in
tasks/QUEUE.md. Four or five sessions can run at once without colliding.


WHAT IT WILL ASK YOU FOR
------------------------
Only these. Everything else runs unattended:

1. New papers since last time — PDFs filed as in AddArticle.txt, plus title,
   authors, venue, year.
2. Anything else that changed — new group members, a paper that moved from
   arXiv to a venue, a repo renamed or newly public.
3. Approval for any batch classification job over $20. It will show you the
   dry-run number first.
4. Whether you want a full-text login sitting this round (see below).
5. A spot check of the first wave of summaries, and a look at the rendered
   pages before cutover.

Expect the whole thing to take a day or two of wall-clock, mostly unattended.


THE FULL-TEXT SITTING — OPTIONAL, AND WORTH UNDERSTANDING
---------------------------------------------------------
Free sources yield about 8% of paywalled papers; that is the bot-wall
ceiling, not a bug. Reading the actual full text flips roughly two thirds of
the prior judgments, so it materially improves the reception data.

Getting it requires you: a logged-in browser, on the MIT proxy hosts, running
a paste-in script that downloads PDFs at human pace. Publisher facts learned
the hard way, all recorded in the playbook:

  - ACM must be reached through dl-acm-org.libproxy.mit.edu. Direct
    dl.acm.org redirects PDFs to the abstract page.
  - Springer reference-work entries often have no PDF at all.
  - The runner must read the PDF link off the landing page rather than
    assume a URL shape.

Skip it when the remaining tail is small — a few dozen papers is not worth
an evening.


SCHEDULING IT AUTOMATICALLY
---------------------------
Claude can run this on a timer, two ways:

  Cowork (easiest): open Claude Desktop, start a Cowork task pointed at the
  repo, type /schedule, and answer the prompts. Scheduled Cowork tasks run
  in the cloud, so your laptop can be closed. Available on all paid plans.

  Claude Code: go to claude.ai/code/scheduled, or use the Scheduled panel in
  the desktop app, and save the prompt with a cadence.

A caution worth taking seriously: do not schedule the whole refresh
unattended. The phases that need your judgment — summary voice, identity
calls, cost approvals — are exactly the ones that go quietly wrong without
you. What works well on a timer is the PREPARATION:

    Every first Monday, in ~/workspace/nextgen: run phases 1 and 2 of
    tasks/HARVEST-AND-UPDATE.md only — identifiers and the citations
    harvest. Do not classify, do not write summaries, do not touch the
    site. Report what is new and what it would cost to process.

Then you read the report and decide whether to run the rest. The expensive,
judgment-heavy work stays on your say-so; the fetching happens while you
sleep.


WHAT NOT TO REDO
----------------
Recorded in tasks/QUEUE.md so an agent does not reopen them:
  - the abandoned sitting-3 tail
  - GitHub handles held as unverified pending a second signal
  - the frozen pilot classifications
